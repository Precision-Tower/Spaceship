## Gravitus Principia: 
Re-Evaluating Gravity through Inertia and Computable Axioms

Abstract
Gravitus Principia is a machine-executable axiomatic framework designed to redefine gravitational understanding through the lens of inertia, resistance, and explicitly computable relationships. Where classical physics observes, Gravitus simulates. Where theory speculates, Gravitus defines. This system does not rely on borrowed equations from legacy models but constructs its own semantic and computational environment rooted in definable, causal structure.

1. Historical Context: Newton’s Argument with Gravity
Isaac Newton, the father of classical mechanics, provided the mathematical structure for understanding force, mass, and acceleration. His inverse-square law precisely described gravitational attraction, yet he never explained what gravity was—only how it behaved. He defined inertia as an object’s resistance to a change in motion and its tendency to conserve that motion once initiated: the principle now called momentum conservation.

Newton implicitly argued that inertia held the key to gravity’s fundamental value. However, the necessary framework—concepts like spacetime and field tension—did not yet exist. He could describe effects but lacked the tools to express underlying causes in a computational language.

2. Einstein’s Shift: Gravity as Geometry
Centuries later, Einstein reframed gravity as a property of spacetime geometry: “Gravity is not a force—it is the curvature of spacetime.” This statement is logically consistent with Newton’s original equations. If force = mass × acceleration (f = ma), then no mass implies no force—thus no weight.

Einstein’s redefinition clarified the shape of the gravitational field but didn’t displace the usefulness of Newton’s formulations in computing effects within that field. The question then becomes:

Is gravity a force we experience or a condition we interpret through mass?

We experience weight only when mass interacts with gravitational potential. This paradox—of gravity as both presence and absence—becomes a key premise in Gravitus Principia.

3. The Gravitus Framework: Axiomatic Foundations
Gravitus Principia defines gravitational behavior through a new axiomatic structure:

Core Axiom:
Potential − Resistance = Force
P − r = f

In this system:
Potential (P) is a field-specific constant (e.g., 9.8 m/s).
Resistance (r) is any opposing interaction (including spatial medium, buoyancy, or inertia).
Force (f) is the result of potential diminished by resistance, whether at rest (weight) or in motion (impact).

Sample Application:
Weight = 9.8 m/s × mass − r

Where r may be a defined input, a contextual variable, or an unknown to be solved.

If we take Newton's f = ma and interpret acceleration as resolved acceleration (a_resolved), then:
v = a_resolved
f = m × v

This frames force as the impact event of mass resolving potential against resistance—a more causal interpretation than continuous force.

4. Redefining Gravity as Inertia over Resistance
We propose that gravity is a product of inertia resolved through a medium of resistance:

Gravity Law:
I / r = g

Where:
I = inertia (mass × velocity)
r = resistance medium (e.g., atmospheric density, field compression)
g = experienced gravitational potential (e.g., 9.8 m/s² on Earth)

In this model, gravity is not imposed; it is emergent from the inertia of a planetary body interacting with spacetime resistance.
