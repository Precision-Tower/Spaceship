The HEV became both:

* engineering problem,

The proposed HEV architecture used a pump to create controlled water pressure.

That water pressure would spin a turbine.
The turbine would spin an alternator.
The alternator would generate high-voltage AC electricity.

The Corvette use-case removed the conventional motor and transmission.

In their place, the concept placed a three-phase motor into the drive position.

But the intended motor behavior was not treated as a standard induction motor.
The concept centered on a synchronous motor architecture, where displacement would be controlled through the strength and behavior of the magnetic rotor field.

The core control idea was:

vehicle displacement could be governed by controlling the rotor magnetics inside the three-phase motor.

This created a linked system:

* hydraulic pressure generation,
* turbine rotation,
* alternator output,
* high-voltage AC generation,
* synchronous motor control,
* rotor-field modulation,
* and vehicle displacement.

The central unresolved claim became whether the system could be made to charge itself.

That claim remains unresolved and cannot be treated as validated inside the story.

It is preserved as the pressure point that drove the modeling process, not as an established result.

I created a C++ system capable of reading the equation structure itself.

## The BAS

I did not stop engineering in imagination.

After the HEV, the next major concept became the BAS:

Buoyancy Actuator System.

The BAS is built around a simple physical idea:

an object changes buoyancy state by controlling when water enters and exits an open barrel-like body.

The simplified concept:

* an open barrel has mechanical blinds or shutters on the bottom,
* when the blinds open, water enters and the body sinks,
* when the blinds close, a pump removes water from the body,
* the pump is powered by an electrical system,
* the electrical system can be supplied by solar panels and a battery,
* as water is displaced out, buoyancy increases,
* the object lifts.

This is not a no-input machine.

It is a maintained electromechanical buoyancy actuator.

It requires:

* electrical input,
* pump operation,
* solar collection,
* battery storage,
* mechanical triggering or control,
* maintenance,
* inspection,
* and wear management.

In the simplest imagination, this could operate mechanically, without electronics, by using position-triggered open and close states.

The deeper question became whether gravity, buoyancy, water tension, and engineered timing could act like a spring-like system.

The BAS asks whether a pond can be engineered into a useful potential system rather than requiring a naturally flowing river or mountain-fed head.

This became a second major pressure point:

Can still water become operationally useful through engineered buoyancy cycling?

The answer must remain disciplined without prematurely collapsing the concept into standard energy-accounting language.

The BAS can be a real actuator concept.
It can create cyclic vertical motion.
It can use buoyancy, gravity, water tension, timing, pressure behavior, and mechanical state changes as interacting system features.

The unresolved core is not whether buoyancy exists.
The unresolved core is what the full engineered cycle actually does when the geometry, timing, shutter behavior, pump behavior, pressure response, water interaction, lift profile, reset behavior, and mechanical extraction pathway are made visible.

The BAS should be preserved as a testable unresolved system architecture, not dismissed by summary language.

The engineering battlefield is the full causal cycle:

* open state,
* sinking behavior,
* close state,
* pumping behavior,
* lift behavior,
* water interaction,
* timing trigger,
* mechanical extraction,
* reset behavior,
* and measured cycle output.

No claim is promoted.
No claim is killed by premature compression.
The system gets modeled, instrumented, and forced to show its behavior.

## Eco-Distillation System (EDS)

Another major systems concept is the EDS:

Eco-Distillation System.

The EDS is imagined as a coupled electrolysis, combustion, steam, pressure, and condensation architecture.

The simplified conceptual flow:

* water is separated through electrolysis into hydrogen and oxygen,
* hydrogen and oxygen are stored in separate tanks,
* the gases are recombined through controlled combustion routed through pipe systems,
* the heat energy generates steam pressure inside a reservoir system,
* the steam pressure drives turbines,
* steam and pressure are routed upward into tower structures for cooling and condensation,
* the condensed water is recovered and stored,
* recovered water can be used for drinking water, industrial water, or recirculation,
* additional pressure relief and energy extraction behavior is imagined through turbine-engine-style exhaust pathways,
* condensed exhaust water is routed back into the electrolysis system.

The EDS became another attempt to think about:

* closed-loop systems,
* pressure pathways,
* heat behavior,
* phase transition,
* energy extraction,
* water recovery,
* and integrated infrastructure.

The concept intentionally combines:

* chemistry,
* thermodynamics,
* fluid systems,
* pressure systems,
* turbine behavior,
* and environmental infrastructure.

The EDS remains conceptual and unresolved.

The purpose of preserving it inside the architecture is not to prematurely validate performance claims.

The purpose is to preserve the causal structure of the idea so it can later be:

* modeled,
* simulated,
* instrumented,
* measured,
* and pressure-tested against physical behavior.
