## Kinetic Energy Model: Categories and Definitions
These categories define the primary domains where kinetic resolution occurs, each adhering to the fundamental p-r=d axiom and specific domain integrity principles.

## Mass-Centric Systems (MC)
Mass-Centric Systems are domains where the primary causal interactions involve the properties and motion of mass, understood as structured field embodiments.

## Mechanical Energy (ME):
The resolution of causal potential into displacement specifically within systems primarily governed by the properties and motion of mass. Mechanical Energy describes the dynamic relationship where accumulated tension (potential) overcomes inertial or structural resistance to produce either linear (translational) or angular (rotational) displacement of mass. It encompasses both the capacity for motion inherent in a mass's configuration (e.g., a raised object's gravitational potential resolved as displacement upon release) and the ongoing resolution of potential as sustained motion.
## Linear Mechanical Energy (ML):
The relationship where an applied potential (e.g., Force, understood as resisted potential or accumulated tension) resolves through the linear displacement of a mass against inertial or environmental resistance. It characterizes motion along a single dimension.
## Angular Mechanical Energy (MA):
The relationship where a rotational potential (e.g., Torque, understood as rotational tension) resolves through the angular displacement (rotational motion) of a mass around an axis, overcoming rotational or structural resistance. It describes the rotational resolution of potential.
## Fluid Dynamic (FD):
The relationship between causal potential and displacement as expressed through the motion or configuration of fluids, which are structured field embodiments of mass. Fluid Dynamic energy manifests as the resolution of potential into pressure, flow rate, and buoyancy as fluids overcome internal (viscosity) or external resistance.
## Ambient Fluid Energy (FA):
The resolution of fluid potential (e.g., fluid momentum, representing the resolved inertial potential of the fluid) into linear displacement (flow rate) within an unconfined or ambient environment, overcoming environmental resistance (e.g., air resistance, channel friction).
## Pneumatic Energy (FP):
The resolution of stored potential in a gas (e.g., compressed gas pressure, which is accumulated tension within the gas's field structure) into displacement, manifesting as expansion or exerted force, as it overcomes resistance (e.g., a container wall, external pressure).
## Hydraulic Energy (FH):
The resolution of stored potential in a liquid (e.g., liquid pressure, which is accumulated tension within the liquid's field structure) into displacement, manifesting as flow or the performance of mechanical work, as it overcomes resistance.
## Buoyancy (Bc):
The resolution of potential (e.g., a density difference, which creates a differential pressure potential within a fluid) into upward displacement (buoyancy force) of a mass within a fluid medium, specifically within a gravitational field (understood as a causal field pressure) as it overcomes the resistance of the surrounding fluid and the gravitational field's influence on the immersed mass.

## Field-Centric Systems (FC)
Field-Centric Systems are domains where causal influence is transmitted through continuous spatial structures (fields) that often exist independently of or are shaped by mass.

## Magnetic Field Potential (Fx):
The relationship between causal potential and displacement specifically within the continuous spatial structures of magnetic fields. It describes how the inherent tension or configuration of aligned Ether (magnetic field potential) resolves into displacement, which manifests as attractive or repulsive field geometry and influences conductive structures by altering their resistance conditions (e.g., inducing current without electron transport). It is the dynamic expression of a magnet's self-sustaining field geometry.
## Electricity (Ec):
The resolution of electric potential through structured mass, manifested as the displacement of electric field lines. It describes how magnetic field tension resolves through a conductor's electron structure, which acts as a reconfigurable field lattice, enabling the propagation of causal field tension (current) without the bulk motion of electrons. Electricity is thus a field-level resolution, not a particle flow.
## Alternating Current (AC):
A form of Electricity where the causal potential (e.g., Voltage, understood as electric field tension) undergoes cyclical reversal, resulting in bidirectional harmonic excitation and displacement of electric field lines within a conductor. Electrons within the conductive material shift alignment without net movement, acting as a field harmonic oscillator that recursively absorbs and re-releases field potential across the structure, with no net mass displacement.
## Direct Current (DC):
A form of Electricity where a continuous electric potential difference (e.g., Voltage) resolves through a conductor, causing unidirectional displacement of electric field lines. Electrons structurally realign under this sustained field pressure, allowing the field tension to propagate directionally as a sustained displacement (current) without the bulk motion of electrons, as resistance holds the field configuration.
## Electromagnetic Wave (EMW):
The relationship between causal potential and displacement as expressed through the recursive field excitation of the Ether substrate. Electromagnetic waves represent the self-sustaining transfer of displacement, where oscillating magnetic field tension induces electric tension, and vice versa, propagating as a structured tension wave through the Ether without requiring particles or a material medium. Light is an observable manifestation of this process at a specific frequency range.
## Thermodynamics (TE)
Thermodynamics, within the Kinetic Energy Model, describes the resolution of thermal potential inherent in matter's field structure. The relationship between thermal potential and displacement, expressed through the resolution of a Thermal Field. Thermodynamics in this model describes how the inherent potential within matter's thermal field resolves as displacement (e.g., expansion, contraction, phase change, buoyancy) when overcoming environmental or internal resistance, rather than solely focusing on heat transfer as a conserved quantity.
