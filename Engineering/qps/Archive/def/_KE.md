## Kinetic Energy Model: Core Definitions
## I. Universal Causal Terms (Derived from Law 1: Causality)
These terms define the fundamental components of all kinetic events, as expressed in the universal Law of Causality (p−r=d).
## Causal Retraction
The fundamental, primordial causal potential of the universe. It is the initial, cold contraction of the Ether in a state of nothingness, from which all subsequent observable structure and fields arise. It is not a field itself, but the foundational impulse that leads to the emergence of structure.0
## Potential (p)
Causal readiness — a configuration of force constrained by structure. It becomes expressed only upon release through displacement.
## Resistance (r)
Any structural, inertial, or field-based opposition to potential or flow. It is the necessary boundary condition for potential to exist.
-In Field-Centric Systems (p−r=d): r is the resistance to the propagation of the constant potential.
-In Mass-Centric Systems (mv−r=f_impact): r represents the total momentum dissipated by the resistance over the duration of the interaction (e.g., deceleration due to viscosity). It is a quality of time, reflecting the cumulative effect of opposition over an interval.
## Displacement (d)
The rate of inertial displacement (d=distance / time). Displacement is the continuation of resolved potential through a medium. Displacement exists only after resistance has been overcome and displacement is unbroken. Displacement is the universal output of kinetic resolution. All causal potential, once resolved through resistance, results in displacement — whether as motion of mass or propagation of field.
## Energy (e)
The interchangeability between potential and displacement. Energy is not a substance; it is the resolution of causal potential through structure. If potential can resolve as displacement it is conserved. Displacement experiencing resistance will lose potential, via deceleration or impact.
## Time (t)
The relational metric that measures the unfolding of causal displacement, existing only as the interval between the initiation of potential and the realization of displacement.
## Volume(V):
The three-dimensional extent of space occupied by a field, fluid, or mass.
Volume (V) represents a container of potential (p) or displacement (d) and is a critical factor in determining the density of mass or the pressure of fluids within that space.
It is a geometric boundary or capacity, not a rate.
## Density(D):
The measure of concentrated potential or causal influence per unit volume within a field or structured mass.
In field-centric systems, it describes the spatial concentration of field tension or potential.
In mass-centric systems, it manifests as mass density (m/V), representing the concentration of structured field embodiment within a given volume.
Density is a primary factor in determining the local capacity for causal interaction and resistance.
## Pressure(P)
The distribution of causal tension or potential (p) over a defined area.
It represents the accumulated force or concentrated potential within a system, seeking to resolve as displacement (d) against resistance (r).
Pressure is a fundamental form of potential that drives resolution across both fluidic and certain field interactions.
Its specific manifestation (e.g., Force/Area in hydraulics, field expansion tension in pneumatics) is context-dependent.


## II. Mass-Centric Terms (Derived from Law 2: Conservation of Momentum)
These terms define the components of kinetic events specifically within systems involving mass and motion, as expressed in the Law of Mass-Motion Resolution 
(f=ma and mv−r=f_impact).
## Mass (m):
Mass is the measure of structural inertia — the resistance to change in motion. It's not a fundamental substance, but rather the structural manifestation of energy, representing a specific configuration where potential (p) is held in equilibrium with displacement (d). This means mass embodies the relationship p=d, where potential is intrinsically balanced by its structural displacement at a given state.
More simply, mass is a structured field embodiment that inherently obstructs displacement in any form. Whether it's gas, liquid, or solid, mass represents a stable geometry of fields where internal potential (p) is balanced by its inherent structural resistance to change (d), thus requiring external potential to induce further displacement.
## Substance
The aggregate forms of mass that emerge from the fusion and reconfiguration of more fundamental field structures (e.g., Hydrogen). Substance specifically refers to the heavier elements formed by nuclear fusion or other complex field interactions, representing more densely packed and intricately structured manifestations of mass. Unlike fundamental field geometries, substances possess increased structural complexity and higher internal resistance to displacement, acting as more consolidated forms of resolved potential.
## Inertia (i)
The structural resistance to change in motion. It is the mass-based measure of how much force is required to change velocity. Inertia is not a force; it is the property of mass that resists acceleration/deceleration. Once acceleration is resolved, inertia becomes momentum.
-It is easier to keep a vehicle in motion than to start it from rest.
## Velocity (v)
The rate of displacement over time.
## Acceleration (a)
The rate of increased displacement over time by the amount of elapsed time (a=distance / time^2).
## Force (f)
The active expression of resisted potential that has not yet fully resolved as velocity. It manifests as a push or pull, representing a structural buildup of causal tension acting against resistance.
---
When resistance is high, potential accumulates instead of resolving. This buildup is experienced as force (the push/pull). Once resistance lowers, this accumulated potential resolves into displacement — velocity — and becomes momentum.
A falling object does not possess force applied to it throughout its fall; rather, its potential (gravity) resolves through decreasing resistance (air, inertia) to become momentum. Force (as an impact) only manifests when this momentum encounters a final, significant resistance that halts the motion.
- In the KE model:
Force is not the cause of motion, but the active manifestation of tension when motion is withheld or when momentum is resolved against resistance.
Force is the action of potential against resistance, or the resultant impact of resolved momentum.
Momentum is the resolved form of that tension once displacement is achieved.
---
In the context of f=ma: Force is the accumulation of potential over time that causes acceleration on a mass.
In the context of mv−r=f 
- impact: Force is the remaining momentum at the point of full resolution (e.g., impact), which then directly expresses as the Impact Force. Force does not exist until there is a point of impact; it is conserved as momentum until that point.
## Momentum (mv) 
The resolved state of inertial potential in motion. It is the continuation of mass displacement once acceleration (resolution of initial potential) has occurred and inertia has been overcome. Momentum is universally conserved within a system, representing the sustained resolution of potential in mass-centric interactions until met by significant resistance.
