1. Purpose of the PMA Manual Generation System

The PMA Manual Generation System provides a redundant, human-powered energy source for the sargassum processing project. It is designed to ensure that critical monitoring, containment, and recovery systems remain functional during periods of low solar yield or primary battery exhaustion.

2. Operational Role

The system serves as a secondary power layer. Its primary roles include:

Emergency Recovery: Restoring power to dead-state battery banks or capacitors to reactivate sensors and communication tools.

Auxiliary Charging: Supplementing solar input during peak demand or low-light conditions to maintain or recover battery charge levels.

Low-Energy Resilience: Providing a baseline of operational capability that is independent of environmental conditions or fuel logistics.

3. Relationship to Primary Support Vessel

The PMA system is integrated into the Primary Support Vessel's power architecture:

Strategic Placement: Mounted in accessible work zones to allow personnel to provide manual input while monitoring process gauges or safety indicators.

System Interface: Wired into the vessel’s central power bus or dedicated emergency charging circuits.

4. Relationship to Solar and Battery Systems

The PMA system is a fallback and supplement, not the primary source:

Solar Primary: Solar arrays remain the primary renewable energy and charging source for high-draw activities (such as electrolysis).

Auxiliary Assistance: The PMA system provides auxiliary charging assistance to the vessel battery systems during low-solar or emergency conditions.

Throughput Limitation: The PMA is not intended to serve as the primary continuous charging source for full operational processing throughput.

5. Manual Input Philosophy

Manual generation is designed to be a sustainable task for a single operator:

Human-Scale Design: Utilizing hand-cranks, pedal-drives, or lever-actions that allow for extended operation without excessive fatigue.

Tactile Feedback: Providing the operator with physical awareness of the electrical load and mechanical state of the system.

6. Gear Reduction and Mechanical Advantage Concept

To optimize human input for electrical generation:

Efficiency: Utilizing high-ratio gearboxes or belt drives to convert low-RPM manual movement into the higher RPMs required by the PMA.

Adjustability: [UNRESOLVED ENGINEERING VARIABLE] – Potential for variable gear ratios to match the strength and stamina of different operators.

7. Flywheel Smoothing Concept

[HYPOTHESIS]
A flywheel may be integrated into the mechanical drive to stabilize manual input.

Rotational Inertia: Smoothing out the pulsed nature of human mechanical input to provide a more consistent RPM to the alternator.

Safety: Requiring a robust enclosure to protect personnel from high-speed rotating mass.

8. PMA Generation Concept

The Permanent Magnet Alternator (PMA) is selected for its robust simplicity:

Low Maintenance: PMAs lack brushes or slip rings, reducing the number of high-wear parts in a marine environment.

Instant Start: Capability to generate current at lower RPMs compared to traditional wound-field alternators.

9. Capacitor Charging Philosophy

[ESTIMATED BEHAVIOR]
The system utilizes a single shared capacitor bank as an intermediate energy buffer.

Burst Power: Capacitors can store manual energy for rapid delivery to low-power electronics or solenoid-actuated valves.

Electrolysis Workflow Support: The capacitor bank is intended for controlled discharge support into either the hydroxide electrolysis workflow or the sulfur recovery/reclaim electrolysis workflow, depending on operational selection.

10. Battery Charging Support Philosophy

The PMA system provides targeted support for vessel batteries:

State-of-Charge Maintenance: Maintaining minimum operational battery states to prevent degradation or loss of critical monitoring.

Emergency Support Charging: Bringing a depleted battery back to a voltage level where primary charge controllers can resume solar charging.

Integration Readiness: The project architecture includes the necessary electrical pathways for auxiliary PMA-assisted battery charging integration.

11. Emergency Power Recovery Use

In a total power failure ("Black Start" scenario), the manual system is the primary recovery tool:

Containment Monitoring: Powering gas sensors and isolation valve controllers to verify system safety.

Communication: Restoring power to handheld radios or satellite links to coordinate recovery with shoreside teams.

The PMA Flywheel Manual Generator is intended to charge the electrolysis capacitor bank used for Capacitor-Buffered Electrolysis Delivery:

Delivery Boundary: Raw unbuffered handcrank output is not the electrolysis delivery method. Electrolysis delivery occurs through the capacitor bank, controlled power electronics, isolation, and discharge-control hardware.
