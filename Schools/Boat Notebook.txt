2. Current System Ruling

The core architectural directive of this vessel is preserved from our governing rulebook:

The boat is a clean, inspectable, ballast-testable support raft, not a processing vessel.

Accordingly, all designs logged in this notebook focus on organizing clean-side operations, logic controls, electrical containment candidates, subject to subsystem validation, and modular operator pathways. It excludes any raw biomass handling, anaerobic digestion structures, or uncontained fluid flows.

Every layer, section, and weight calculation must preserve visual inspectability of all joints, straps, and flotation units.

Layer Details:

Layer 0: Static Waterline / Fluid Boundary

Description: Marine boundary layer.

Modeling Targets: Expected trim and list response to be observed during flotation testing.

Risk/Constraint: No uncontained process fluid or battery compartment is permitted to drop into direct contact with Layer 0.

Layer 1: Flotation Array (HDPE Drum Rows)

Description: Symmetrical rows of HDPE drum candidates or other validated flotation units.

Modeling Targets: Continuous visual contact along the entire length of the row to check for leaks, outer-wall chafing, or hull degradation.

Risk/Constraint: Flotation units must remain entirely un-penetrated by permanent structural fasteners.

Layer 2: Drum Capture & Restraint Frame

Description: Longitudinal cradle rails, side stops, and end-stops designed to contain Layer 1. Includes adjustable anti-slip restraint straps.

Modeling Targets: Strap tension remains manually checkable. Strap creep must remain observable via witness marks.

Risk/Constraint: Failure of restraint straps directly threatens buoyant stability; must remain highly visible from above.

Layer 3: PVC Distributed Short-Span Truss Chassis

Description: Distributed short-span PVC truss network composed of repeatable, braced structural bays.

Modeling Targets: Translates deck forces down to the drum cradle frames. Uses visible joint markers [CANDIDATE PLACEHOLDER — PENDING VALIDATION].

Risk/Constraint: Subject to deflection and joint shear testing. Long-term creep of PVC members under persistent load remains a critical unresolved risk.

Layer 4: Removable Deck Surface (Panels / Grating)

Description: Candidate grating or removable deck panels [PENDING VALIDATION].

Modeling Targets: Must feature removable access features [PENDING VALIDATION] for rapid top-down removal.

Risk/Constraint: Undergoes deck flex testing to confirm support span integrity. Grating must not block the line of sight to underlying Layer 3 joints or Layer 2 straps. Does not establish validated walking-surface safety prior to physical testing.

Layer 5: Modular Equipment Placement / Utility Bays (Skids)

Description: Skid-mounted equipment modules sitting in designated, low-mount Port and Starboard utility bays flanking the central centerline aisle.

Modeling Targets: Candidate zones for battery controls, electrolysis skids, and monitoring stations. Includes the sacrificial clean/dirty side interface modules at the deck edge.

Risk/Constraint: All candidate mass above must be distributed to align with load-spreader pads and underlying truss nodes to prevent point-loading deck grating directly.

A. Access / Inspection Contradictions to Mine

Contradiction

Why It Matters

Bracing density vs. inspection visibility

Diagonal structural members keep the frame stiff but can block the direct vertical line-of-sight required to check flotation restraints and lower joint nodes.

Deck panel size vs. manual handling access

Larger panels reduce the number of seam supports, but they increase manual handling weight and lift-clearance, potentially forcing the operator to step off the central aisle.

Equipment skid footprints vs. under-deck accessibility

Dense skid landing zones are necessary to distribute payload weight, but permanent skid frames can trap the removable deck panels beneath them, creating hidden failure zones.

Splash/vent shielding vs. rapid leak detection

Protective covers prevent operator chemical contact and splash exposure, but they conceal tube joins, fittings, and gravity drip loops from direct visual path monitoring.

A. Replaceability Contradictions to Mine

Contradiction

Why It Matters

Replaceability vs. Structural Rigidity

Securing components with quick-release fasteners makes field swaps simple but can compromise primary truss stiffness and lead to connection pin loosening under cyclic loads.

Clearance vs. Skid Footprints

Dense payload skids distribute heavy loads but can trap underlying removable deck panels and diagonal braces, preventing access to flotation without crane lifting.

Quick-disconnect accessibility vs. Spill containment

Splash and wind shields prevent operator exposure and drip pathways but can physically block hands and tools during emergency manual replacement.

Flotation capture vs. replacement speed

Cradle rails and high-tension straps hold drum rows completely secure but require significant clearance and manual manipulation to slide out under water.

B. Replaceability Priority Classes

emergency removal candidate

Description: Components requiring rapid, immediate alternative routing or safety decoupling context, where immediate separation of functional loops is simulated.

frequent replacement candidate

Description: Components subject to regular visual checking and physical swap testing under normal layout iteration, without requiring structural teardowns.

post-event replacement candidate

Description: Components designed to be replaced following structural contact, tow strain events, or dirty-side boundary failures.

periodic replacement candidate

Description: Components requiring baseline operational halting and manual weight unloading for safe mock swap verification.

major-teardown replacement candidate

Description: High-consequence structural frames or backbone components requiring complete system suspension or partial frame deconstruction to swap.

not-yet-replaceable / unresolved

Description: Unresolved structural nodes or system parameters where no removal path can be modeled until physical mockup measurements are taken.

Note: No class defines maintenance schedules, response times, personnel requirements, tool requirements, field-service certification, or validated service method.
